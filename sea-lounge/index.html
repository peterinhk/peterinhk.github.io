Where We Go

Stops on the Sound

Every charter is built out of these. Tell us which ones matter to you when you book and we’ll shape the run around them — most days are three stops, and the boat is small enough to change plan when the weather says so.

Stingray City & the Sandbar

NORTH SOUND · WAIST-DEEP · SUITS EVERYONE

A sandbar in the middle of the Sound where southern stingrays have gathered for generations — the habit started with fishermen cleaning their catch here on the way in. You stand on the sand rather than swim, which makes it the one stop that works for everybody aboard, confident swimmer or not.

Coral Gardens

MINUTES FROM THE SANDBAR · SHALLOW

Barely any deeper than the Sandbar and usually just as calm. Soft coral, sergeant majors and the odd parrotfish, sheltered enough that first-time snorkellers get their heads down within a minute of going in.

Starfish Point

NORTH SHORE · ANKLE-DEEP · WALK IN

A quiet stretch of the north shore where red cushion sea stars sit in ankle-deep water. Look at them, photograph them, and leave them exactly where they are — lifting one out of the water can kill it, and our crew will ask you not to.

Rum Point

ACROSS THE SOUND · BEACH STOP

The north side’s easy afternoon: casuarina shade, hammocks, calm water straight off the sand, and a bar that has been pouring mudslides for decades. The natural second half of a day that started at the Sandbar.

Kaibo

NORTH SHORE · ARRIVE BY BOAT

A short run along the shore from Rum Point. Beach bar down on the sand, a proper kitchen upstairs, and a dock built for people turning up by boat rather than by road — usually the last stop before the run home.

These are public stops on North Sound, shared with every other charter on the water — we don’t own them and neither does anyone else.     From the Log

    The Sea Lounge

    The Sea Lounge sits on North Sound, Grand Cayman — half watersports outfit, half bar & grill, built for the stretch of afternoon between a snorkel trip and a sunset cruise. We run the Sandbar, the reef and the north shore by day and keep the kitchen open for whoever's on the dock, tourist or local.

    Two sailings, a small stack of snorkel gear, and a lunch menu built for pickup — that's the whole operation, kept simple on purpose.
